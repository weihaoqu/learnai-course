Week 14: Final Web App Showcase and AI Collaboration Reflection
Slide 1
60-minute final showcase

Show the app, the evidence, and the judgment.

The final presentation is not just a polished demo. Students show the static app, explain how AI helped, identify what they verified, and state what limitations remain.

In class

Present or gallery-walk the final app with evidence from Weeks 10, 11, and 13.

Outcome

A Final Showcase Packet plus peer feedback and final reflection.

Boundary

Prototype showcase only. No credentials, private data, hidden answer keys, or unsupported high-stakes claims.

Pacing: 3 minutes. Make evidence and judgment as visible as the app interface.
3-8 min

Choose the showcase mode by class size.

The format should protect time for evidence, feedback, and post-test closure.

Small class

4-minute presentation plus 2-minute questions per student or team.

Medium class

3-minute demos in groups, then rotating peer feedback.

Large class

Gallery walk: students post links/packets, reviewers rotate with feedback cards.

8-14 min

Every final demo follows the same evidence arc.

The app is the center, but the presentation also shows the design process and responsible AI use.

Problem

Target user and need.

Demo

Final app link or fallback.

Design

One Week 8-10 decision.

Evidence

One Week 11 source trail.

Safety

One Week 13 limitation.

Next

One realistic improvement.

Fallback is valid

Acceptable fallback formats: local HTML file, zipped static site, screenshot or video walkthrough, or hosted preview without credentials. If the public link contains a secret or private-data issue, do not present it.

14-22 min

Grade evidence and judgment, not only polish.

The rubric should reward a working artifact, but also the process evidence that makes AI-assisted work trustworthy.

22-28 min

Check presentation readiness before speaking.

Click the items that are ready. Any unchecked item becomes a fallback or limitation note.

Showcase tool

Use a timer that protects the evidence trail.

The presentation should not spend all its time on the interface. Keep time for source, safety, and reflection.

Reflection builder

Disclosure is not a confession. It is professional documentation of collaboration, review, and accountability.

Peer review

Keep comments specific, constructive, and tied to visible evidence.

Course close

Finish with reflection and the post-test if active.

If the research assessment workflow is active for this section, students complete the post-test after the final showcase. If it is inactive, use the same time for final reflection.

Active research workflow

Use the course hub post-test link. Students should complete it after presentations, following the instructor's timing instructions.

Inactive research workflow

Do not invent a post-test requirement. Ask students to submit a final reflection instead.

Final reflection prompt

What can you now build with AI that you could not build before, and what responsibility do you still carry as the human?

Final boundary

A final project is a prototype and learning artifact, not proof of production readiness, security, or real-world effectiveness.
