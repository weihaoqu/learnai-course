Week 4: Writing and Word Documents
Slide 1
60-minute writing lab

AI writing is useful when revision stays reviewable.

Today we use AI to improve professional writing without losing student voice, evidence, privacy, or responsibility. The output is not a prettier paragraph. The output is a traceable revision packet.

In class

Build a prompt brief, revise a professional message, and decide which suggestions to accept or reject.

Tools

Copilot in Word is the main path. Claude for Word is encouraged where available. Normal AI chat plus a change log is the fallback.

Artifact

Submit a Reviewable Revision Packet with original text, prompt, revision, checks, disclosure, and rejected suggestions.

Pacing: 4 minutes. Connect back to Week 3: allowed use still needs data boundaries, verification, and disclosure.

32-42 min

Lab 1: turn rough text into a professional message.

Rough draft

hi professor i missed the quiz because i had stuff going on can i make it up soon i really need points for my grade thanks

Task: revise for a respectful meeting request. Do not invent an excuse. Keep the student's responsibility visible.

Review lens

i had stuff going on I was unable to attend class and should have contacted you sooner

Would you be willing to meet during office hours so I can ask whether any make-up option is available?

Before accepting AI's revision

  • Does it preserve the real facts?
  • Does it keep the student's voice and responsibility?
  • Does it avoid pressuring the reader?
  • Does it ask clearly for the next step?
  • What sentence should be rejected or rewritten?
Pacing: 10 minutes. Have students write one accepted change and one rejected change.
42-46 min

Revision signal: specificity, evidence, and agency.

Polished writing is not automatically good writing. Treat generic AI-like style as a revision signal, not a detector game.

Look for

  • Vague claims with no actor or evidence.
  • Inflated transitions that add no meaning.
  • Confident tone without verified facts.

Repair from verified material

  • Use names, dates, examples, numbers, theories, citations, or observations only when they already appear in your allowed source set.
  • Make the writer or verified source responsible for each claim.
  • If evidence is missing, flag the gap. Do not invent a detail to make the paragraph sound stronger.

Submit judgment

For one important edit, write a short note:

Before -> After -> Why this improved the argument

Decision game

Accept, reject, or revise?

AI suggestions are proposals. Students are responsible for final judgment.

Suggestion 1 of 5

AI adds: "I had a serious family emergency and therefore could not complete the quiz."

Reject when AI...

  • Invents facts, reasons, data, or sources.
  • Changes the promise or request.
  • Sounds unlike the writer.
  • Removes accountability.
  • Uses private or restricted content.

Homework

Due before Week 5.

Submit one reviewable revision

Use a non-sensitive email, memo, or paragraph. Include original text, AI prompt brief, revised version, tracked-changes screenshot or manual change log, verification notes, disclosure, rejected suggestions with rationale, and one Before / After / Why note.

Use approved tools

Use Copilot in Word if available. Claude for Word is encouraged if installed. If neither works, use approved AI chat and a manual change log.
