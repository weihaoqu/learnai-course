Shared teaching language

The 4D framework becomes the course spine.

Use the same four terms in slides, critiques, homework, rubrics, and final presentations. Repetition matters: students should hear the words often enough that they become a design habit.

D

Delegation

What should AI do, what should the student do, and why is that division appropriate?

D

Description

What goal, context, constraints, examples, and output format does the student provide?

D

Discernment

How does the student inspect, test, compare, and revise AI output?

D

Diligence

What privacy, integrity, citation, safety, and disclosure responsibilities does the student handle?

Facilitation map

Where 4D shows up across the 14 weeks.

Weeks 1-3
Introduce AI fluency and complete Framework & Foundations.
Make students use 4D language in short reflections and risk audits.
Weeks 4-6
Apply AI to writing, data, workflows, and media.
Require Description and Discernment checks: prompt clarity, output review, citation/verification.
Week 7
Midterm studio.
Ask students to explain one good delegation choice and one output they rejected or revised.
Weeks 8-10
Build web app concept, interactivity, and complete project.
Use the 4D process log as the project lab notebook.
Week 11
Complete AI Fluency for Students.
Connect learning partner, career planning, and personal AI collaboration policy.
Week 12
Advanced prompting.
Use Description-Discernment loops to improve prompts and evaluate outputs.
Week 13
Security and vulnerability management.
Emphasize Diligence: private data, unsafe code, model overconfidence, and review habits.
Week 14
Final presentation.
Require project demo plus AI-use disclosure, selected process logs, and personal policy reflection.
Assessment design

Grade the collaboration, not only the final product.

Outcome

The visible artifact: document, media piece, app prototype, project, presentation, or career plan.

Process

The 4D evidence: prompts, decisions, tests, revisions, rejected outputs, and safety checks.

Reflection

The student's explanation of what AI helped with, what they changed, and what they remain responsible for.

Compact 4D rubric

Dimension
Developing
Proficient
Strong
Delegation
Uses AI because it is available.
Explains why AI is suitable for part of the task.
Separates AI work from human judgment with clear rationale.
Description
Prompt is vague or missing constraints.
Prompt includes goal, context, and output expectations.
Prompt gives task structure, constraints, examples, and success criteria.
Discernment
Accepts output with little inspection.
Checks output against task requirements.
Tests, compares, revises, and documents what changed.
Diligence
Does not address privacy, integrity, or citation.
Identifies relevant responsibility issues.
Handles disclosure, data boundaries, citations, and safety risks explicitly.
Classroom scripts

Reusable prompts for instructor-led activities.

Prompt repair discussion

Use when students write broad prompts that produce generic output.

Ask: 1. What did you want AI to do? 2. What did you assume AI already knew? 3. What constraint would prevent the wrong output? 4. What would count as a successful answer?

Peer critique protocol

Use before students submit app prompts, writing drafts, media designs, or final project work.

Partner check: - I can name the target user. - I can see what AI was asked to do. - I can identify one missing constraint. - I can explain how the output should be tested. - I can name one risk or responsibility issue.

Instructor interventions

Common student failure patterns.

Prompt is too broad

Intervention: ask for target user, output format, constraints, and one success criterion.

AI output sounds plausible

Intervention: require source checks, comparison, or a small test before acceptance.

Student copies the example

Intervention: make them change user, context, and success criteria before building.

Project scope explodes

Intervention: reduce to one screen, one interaction, one output, and no backend.

Private data enters the prompt

Intervention: replace with fictional or generalized data and document the Diligence choice.

Student hides AI use

Intervention: grade AI-use disclosure as normal professional documentation, not confession.

Adaptation guide

For instructors outside CS.

The course can be adapted if instructors keep the 4D spine and replace the final web app with a discipline-appropriate artifact.

Business

Replace the web app with a workflow, dashboard mockup, client brief, or operations playbook.

Humanities

Replace the web app with an annotated source analysis, writing portfolio, or public-facing exhibit.

Health / Social sciences

Use fictional data and focus on risk communication, citation, consent, and human review.
